#### Summary

Jiaona Zhang lays out a 'company OS' that turns AI adoption from a PM-side experiment into shared operating infrastructure across the org. For AI Ops with non-technical business owners, it also offers a pragmatic rollout path: start with one repo, one ontology, and one high-frequency workflow at a time instead of chasing disconnected AI tools.

### 2. Investing in Mirendil

#### Summary

The piece argues that scaling laws have concentrated frontier AI progress inside a few giant labs, but the next wave of value will come from letting outside engineers and domain experts do real model work instead of only calling APIs. Mirendil's proposed mechanism is a lab-grade research platform whose models are tuned for AI R&D loops: proposing experiments, writing and running code, interpreting results, debugging failures, managing compute, and iterating with less human intervention. a16z uses Cursor's progression from third-party models to open-source Composer models and then frontier pretraining as evidence that owning more of the stack can improve both economics and performance.

#### Why It Matters

For AI PM coaching and portfolio work, this is a useful reframing from 'which model should I use?' to 'which layer of the stack should I own?' For AI Ops, the lesson is similar: long-term advantage will come from systems that encode domain expertise and run controlled experiments, not from bolting a generic assistant onto every workflow.

### 3. Tech interviews with NeetCode - by Gergely Orosz

#### Summary

The interview is less about leetcode tactics and more about what remains durable as coding gets cheaper. NeetCode argues that algorithm-heavy interviews persist because they scale interviewer training and evaluation, not because they perfectly mirror day-to-day engineering, and he uses examples from Amazon, Google, and his own hiring to show why independence, systems thinking, and high agency still separate strong engineers. #### Why It Matters

This is strong coaching material because it gives candidates a cleaner narrative than 'AI changed everything': the bar is shifting from raw coding speed toward judgment, problem framing, and the ability to defend choices. For AI Ops, the parallel is that agents reduce execution cost, so verification, tradeoff management, and ownership become the scarce capabilities.
