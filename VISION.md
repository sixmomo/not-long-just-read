# XHS Ops Codex Vision

## Project Purpose

This folder is the operating system for one Xiaohongshu account:

> 产品 momo / 北美 AI PM coach

The goal is to turn scattered strategy notes, topic ideas, drafts, visuals, and performance reviews into a repeatable XHS operations workflow.

This project should help us:

- Keep account positioning current.
- Generate commercially meaningful topics.
- Write posts in a consistent voice.
- Plan carousel structure and illustration prompts.
- Track post performance over time.
- Run a weekly strategy pivot before creating the next note.

It is not just a content archive. It is a living operations folder for testing, learning, and refining the account.

## Account Scope

For now, this project serves one account only.

The account focuses on:

- North America PM / TPM / AI PM interviews.
- PM case preparation.
- Interview storytelling and positioning.
- AI PM career transition and coaching observations.
- Team-specific problem solving in interviews.
- Translating candidate experience into team-relevant product judgment.

The account should avoid drifting into broad AI prompts, generic productivity, startup diary, or unrelated lifestyle content unless those topics clearly support the PM interview and coaching positioning.

## North Star Priority

The operating priority for this project is:

1. Test commercial topic potential.
2. Build the North America AI PM coach persona.
3. Improve save rate and content usefulness.
4. Grow followers.
5. Drive coaching and consulting leads.
6. Rebuild a consistent publishing rhythm.

This order matters.

We should not chase easy reach if the topic does not test a meaningful coaching need. We should not optimize only for aesthetics if the post does not strengthen the account's commercial positioning. Each post should help answer:

> Would this help a North America professional who wants to transition into AI PM prepare better for interviews, build a stronger AI portfolio, and move faster toward an AI PM offer?

The audience would potentially pay to solve:

- How to transition into AI PM from their current background.
- How to prepare for AI PM interviews.
- How to build a compelling AI project or portfolio.
- How to translate existing work experience into AI PM-relevant stories.
- How to position themselves as a lower-risk AI PM candidate.
- How to improve their odds of getting interviews, passing loops, and receiving AI PM offers.

## Target Audience

The primary audience is:

- Chinese-speaking professionals in North America, or targeting North America roles.
- 24-34-year-old professionals with some work experience, not complete beginners.
- People who want to transition into AI PM faster or improve their odds of getting an AI PM offer.
- PM, TPM, SWE, data, consulting, operations, founder, or other non-standard backgrounds trying to reposition themselves for AI PM.
- Candidates who need AI PM interview guidance, AI project guidance, and portfolio positioning.
- Candidates who prepare a lot but still fail to sound relevant to the target team.

Their core anxieties include:

- Why did I fail the interview?
- What is the interviewer really testing?
- How do I make my experience sound relevant?
- How do I prepare product design / strategy cases?
- How do I know whether the failure was my problem or a market/team variable?
- How do I show AI ability without sounding like I am just showing off buzzwords?
- How can I build a compelling AI project?

## Core Account Promise

The account does not simply provide information. It provides judgment.

The account helps candidates move from:

> "Here is what I know."

to:

> "Here is how I solve this team's problem."

The most important positioning layer is:

> Help North America PM / TPM / AI PM candidates translate their experience, AI ability, and case answers into the problem-solving capability that a target team actually cares about.

## Project Development Phases

### Phase 1: Structured Strategy Knowledge Base

Organize the current account assets into a clear source of truth:

- Account positioning.
- Fan persona.
- Topic matrix.
- Voice corpus.
- Viral post reviews.
- Commercialization assumptions.

The purpose of this phase is to make the account strategy easy to maintain and easy to reuse when producing new notes.

### Phase 2: Semi-Automated Content Pipeline

For each new topic, the project should support a repeatable workflow:

1. Topic input or coaching observation.
2. Commercial hypothesis.
3. Audience pain point.
4. Content angle.
5. Carousel structure.
6. Page-by-page copy.
7. Cover options.
8. Image generation prompts.
9. XHS title, caption, tags, and comment prompt.

The goal is not to remove human judgment. The goal is to make good judgment easier to apply consistently.

### Phase 3: Comment Guidance and Comment Operations

Each post should include an intentional comment strategy before publishing and a comment operations workflow after publishing.

The goal is not only to increase engagement. The goal is to invite the right audience to reveal what they are struggling with, respond in a way that strengthens the account persona, and identify which problems may become coaching offers.

Pre-publish comment guidance can include:

- A question at the end of the caption.
- A pinned comment that invites background sharing.
- A keyword prompt for readers who want a follow-up post.
- A soft diagnostic prompt, such as asking what role, background, or AI project they are preparing.
- A next-post voting prompt.

Post-publish comment operations can include:

- Deciding which comments deserve thoughtful replies.
- Drafting replies in the account voice.
- Categorizing high-frequency questions.
- Identifying potential coaching or consulting leads.
- Turning strong comments into follow-up post ideas.
- Using comments as public proof of audience pain points.
- Writing response-style follow-up posts, such as "上篇有姐妹问..."

The comment workflow should help us identify:

- Which AI PM transition pain points are most urgent.
- Which audience segments are showing up.
- Whether readers are asking for interview help, portfolio help, positioning help, or project ideas.
- Which questions can become the next post.
- Which comments suggest potential coaching or consulting demand.

This phase connects content publishing, audience interaction, and commercial learning.

### Phase 4: Performance Tracking

The user will provide XHS backend screenshots or manually shared numbers.

This project should store performance data in a traceable table, including fields such as:

- Publish date.
- Topic.
- Content pillar.
- Commercial hypothesis.
- Views.
- Likes.
- Saves.
- Comments.
- Shares.
- Follower change.
- Save rate.
- Comment signal.
- Comment guidance used.
- Comment keywords.
- Potential follow-up topics from comments.
- Lead or consultation signal.
- Lessons learned.

Performance should be reviewed in context, not as vanity metrics only.

### Phase 5: Weekly Strategy Pivot

Before creating the next weekly note, we should review the previous post's performance and decide whether to pivot.

The weekly pivot should answer:

- Did the topic validate a commercial coaching need?
- Did the post strengthen the AI PM coach persona?
- Did users save it?
- Did users ask follow-up questions?
- Did the comments reveal a sharper pain point?
- Did the comment guidance attract the right audience signals?
- Should the next post continue, deepen, or change direction?
- Does the account positioning need a small update?

The rhythm is:

> Publish -> collect data -> review performance -> update strategy -> create next note.

### Phase 6: Visual Style System

The visual workflow should start with prompts, not fixed templates.

Initial workflow:

1. Generate several visual directions.
2. Pick the strongest direction manually.
3. Generate cover and interior page prompts.
4. Review readability, mobile fit, and brand consistency.
5. Save strong visual patterns.

Over time, this should become a reusable visual system:

- Cover templates.
- Interior page templates.
- Prompt patterns.
- Color and typography rules.
- Examples of what works and what to avoid.

The visual system should support the account's positioning: sharp, professional, personal, useful, and not like a generic AI template.

## Weekly Operating Workflow

Each weekly content cycle should follow this order:

1. Review the last post's performance.
2. Extract audience and commercial signals.
3. Decide the strategy pivot.
4. Pick the next topic.
5. Define the commercial hypothesis.
6. Draft the carousel structure.
7. Draft copy in the account voice.
8. Design the comment guidance.
9. Generate visual prompts.
10. Prepare title, caption, tags, and comment prompt.
11. After publishing, record performance data.

## Quality Bar

Every post should pass these checks:

- The topic is tied to a real PM / TPM / AI PM interview or career pain.
- The post strengthens the North America AI PM coach persona.
- The post has a clear commercial learning goal.
- The audience can save it for later use.
- The copy sounds like 产品 momo, not a generic content account.
- The visual direction supports the message instead of distracting from it.
- The post contains judgment, not just information.

## Working Principle

The project should stay flexible enough for frequent pivots, but structured enough that each pivot leaves useful residue.

The goal is not to lock the account into a rigid strategy.

The goal is to make every new note slightly smarter than the last one.
